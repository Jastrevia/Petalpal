Cinematic fantasy realism with refined painterly stylisation: a single frame from a high-budget fantasy feature film, shot on a large-format digital cinema camera with soft spherical prime lenses and graded by a feature-film colourist. The world is the Hidden Valley of PetalPal: a secluded high valley of pale ivory limestone softened by moss and ferns, luminous clear turquoise water (jewel-like #5AA4AB deepening to teal), long cascades of lavender-violet wisteria (#A67EB7), drifts of crocus flowers with saffron-gold stamens, sage and moss greens, warm honey-gold accents. Early-morning golden sunlight from the left, gentle volumetric haze and light shafts, cool airy shadows that never go black, soft highlight bloom, natural depth of field, fine film grain. Mood: gentle, welcoming, quietly magical; companionship and belonging, never epic, dark or menacing. Grade: soft contrast, slightly lifted shadows with a hint of teal, warm honey highlights, saturation restrained everywhere except water and blossom. Physically believable materials: wet stone, translucent petals, soft moss, clear refractive water.

THE GRAND REVEAL — PETALPAL WONDERLAND. The valley as the heart of PetalPal, seen from a mossy terrace about six metres above the lake beside a great tree, in a natural cinematic perspective, not a top-down game view; 32mm lens, deep focus with gentle softness in the far distance. Left third, close and grand: the twisting trunk and buttress roots of an immense ancient tree gripping a limestone shelf, its deep teal-green canopy across the top left pouring long lavender wisteria. Grown into the base of the trunk, clearly visible, is the fairy's tiny home: a knee-high round arched door of honey-toned wood with a petal-shaped stained-glass window glowing with warm lamplight, a little hooded roof of turquoise-glazed tiles with upswept eaves, delicate glass lanterns hanging from the roots, root-and-stone stairs winding down to the water and, beside the stairs, a small mailbox on a carved post shaped like a closed crocus bud; crocus clusters by the door show how small it is. Centre right, in the middle distance on a small island in the luminous turquoise lake, stands the pavilion: slender carved pale columns, a two-tiered roof of deep slate-blue tiles with gracefully upswept, curled eaves, a small gold lotus finial, wisteria trailing from the eaves. Arched pale stone bridges and stepping stones link it to the shore, and where the nearest bridge meets the terrace stands a delicate antique gilded-bronze arch of leaf filigree wound with wisteria. Behind the island, upper right, a tall elegant waterfall pours from a cleft in sunlit limestone cliffs into a misty pool, its spray glowing. Along the bottom of the frame the terrace is planted with crocus in lavender, violet and ivory with saffron-gold stamens. Cliffs with hanging gardens, layered misty mountains in golden haze beyond. Low golden morning sun from the left: god rays through the canopy and the spray, warm lamplight glowing in the tree's cool shade, soft mist on the water, petals drifting. Every landmark feels like a real, lived-in place: unmistakably PetalPal, warm, storybook-rich and alive.

No people, no fairies or other characters, no animals in the foreground, no text, letters, signs, logos or watermarks. Avoid: oversaturated neon colours, glitter, sparkle overload, lens-flare streaks, plastic CGI surfaces, video-game or mobile-game rendering, flat digital illustration, cartoon proportions, castles or cities, symmetrical centred composition, mushy undefined foliage, duplicated or impossible waterfalls, warped architecture.
